MARB scoring spec (v0.10)

The canonical, versioned scoring method for the Mechanical Assembly Readiness Benchmark. MARB grades how correctly AI-assisted CAD places the parts of a real machine. It is developed by Sunnyday Technologies and runs on the open-source CADCLAW verification engine.

Read this first. Sunnyday Technologies authored MARB, runs the CADCLAW engine that grades every score, and supplies the reference machine (our own M3-CRETE assembly), which scores the rubric ceiling (0.0 mm gap, 100% oriented) by construction. CADCLAW is the floor and the referee, not a contender. The eight existing frontier cells are dated single runs under v0.9; local and sighted cells are repeat-run medians with spread. New frontier cells require at least three attempted and three graded independent runs. Read all results as bounded observations, not statements about a product's overall quality or readiness.

1. What MARB measures, and why the metric changed

The earlier v0.3 headline was a single buildability score, dominated by interference. It collapsed every run to roughly 12 to 15 out of 100 and barely separated models. For an early-stage test that is the wrong signal. The useful question, and the one that separates models, is: how many parts did the AI place in the correct position and orientation, and are the gaps between parts functionally correct?

MARB v0.10 retains the three v0.9 positional metrics and fixed bands:

  • GAP is the error between the actual and intended interface gap. It is the primary functional score.
  • POS is the position error of each part versus the answer key, after a best-fit rigid alignment.
  • ORIENT is the share of orientation-gradeable parts placed in the correct rotation.

The configured native inventory, interference, and floating-part checks remain as secondary artifact gates; they are no longer the headline. Passing these digital checks does not establish physical assemblability or manufacturability.

2. Tolerance philosophy

Two points define the standard and do not change.

First, digital placement is graded exactly. A perfect digital model has no placement error: a part is either in the correct spot or it is not. Real machines function at fractions of a millimeter, so the bar is tight. A part within 5 mm of its target counts as located; within 0.01 mm counts as exact. The only allowance is floating-point and export noise, which is far tighter than commercial CAD requires. The band is never loosened to favor a model.

Second, manufacturing tolerance is a separate axis: the plus-or-minus of material added or removed when a real part is made from the perfect digital model. That is the purpose of the CADCLAW tolerance gate, and it is not exercised here. Today MARB grades only whether the AI can place the parts correctly. Production tolerance is graded later (see Section 8).

3. The functional-gap metric (GAP)

Absolute coordinates are a proxy. What distinguishes a functional system from a single fused body is the gap at each interface, and GAP grades that directly.

  • Attachment interfaces: intended gap about 0 mm. Parts that bolt or fasten together must touch.
  • Motion interfaces: intended gap about 1 to 2 mm. Parts that move relative to each other, such as wheels in slots or gantry carriages, need a clearance.

A correct functional assembly reproduces the intended gap at each interface. Collapsing every gap to 0 mm fuses the machine into one body; missing the attachment contacts leaves a set of separated parts. GAP maps onto the CADCLAW adjacency check (parts that should touch) and the interference and clearance check (parts that should not overlap and that should clear). Position and orientation are the foundation that GAP builds on.

4. Scales and bands

Every part-level measure is reported at two scales:

  • POS absolute: each part's pose versus the answer key in a common frame, after a best-fit rigid alignment (Kabsch or ICP on label-matched centroids). Runs are authored in different global frames, so the alignment puts them in one frame before scoring.
  • POS relative (neighbor-relative): each part's pose relative to its neighbors. This scale is frame-invariant and separates whether the model understood how parts connect from whether it anchored the whole machine in the right place.

The headline number per scale is the median error: millimeters for position, degrees for orientation. The median drops toward 0 as models improve, and it never requires the band to be loosened. The bands are tight and fixed:

MetricBands
Positionexact at 0.01 mm or less; within 1 mm; located at 5 mm or less; then 25, 50, and 100 mm bins
Orientationexact at 0.5 degrees or less; aligned at 5 degrees or less; slightly off at 15 degrees or less; otherwise wrong

5. How a run is graded

Grading is automated and tool-independent. The grader loads the answer-key STEP and the run's exported STEP with no roles or metadata, labels each solid by its bounding-box signature, rigid-aligns the two assemblies (Kabsch or ICP), matches parts by a linear-assignment solve, and computes the POS bands and median (absolute and relative). A separate pass classifies each answer-key interface as attachment, motion, or standoff and grades the actual gap against the intended gap (GAP). A third pass uses world-axis-aligned bounding-box extents as an orientation proxy and bins each gradeable part as aligned, rotated, or wrong; parts with two near-equal extents are skipped because the proxy cannot distinguish their rotation (ORIENT). The secondary buildability gate runs the native CADCLAW checks: inventory, interference, and floating. The method is versioned with this document; it changes only when this spec changes.

Acceptable solution classes and current limitation

The current M3-CRETE key is one resolver-built geometric answer. Per-label optimal assignment already treats repeated same-label parts as interchangeable. ORIENT skips parts when two near-equal axis-aligned bounding-box extents make rotation unobservable to its current proxy; this is a scoring-observability rule, not a declaration of functional rotation equivalence. Those limited rules do not cover every potentially acceptable topology: GAP uses the selected reference's interface graph, and relative POS uses its neighbour graph. A different handed layout, part count, or interface topology can therefore be penalized by the single-reference comparison, and MARB has not established whether such an alternative is functionally acceptable.

The v0.10 current-kit audit found no independently validated second complete geometric answer class, so none is declared. Potential alternate layouts remain unvalidated scoring cases, not accepted equivalents and not evidence that one feature tree is uniquely valid. If a future task declares multiple resolver-built classes, MARB will select one complete class for the whole grade rather than cherry-pick a different reference for each metric.

At the 5 mm precision a real machine needs, today's frontier AI places about 0% of parts correctly on the absolute scale. The median part is roughly 10 cm off in absolute terms and about 5 cm off relative to its neighbors. Parts land in roughly the correct region, but not at functional precision, and none of these results is buildable yet. The answer-key frame is about 2175 mm wide and the runs' frames are about 2048 mm wide: the models built a slightly smaller machine, which inflates the absolute figures, so the relative metric factors that difference out.

7. Frontier publication policy

The frontier cells published from 2026-05-26 through 2026-06-11 remain dated v0.9 single-run observations. Effective 2026-08-28, a new frontier cell is published only after at least three independent attempts and three gradeable outputs. A provider seed is recorded where available; otherwise MARB records a distinct independent-run ordinal. Each attempt also records a unique run ID and distinct run-log path and SHA-256 digest; each graded output records its STEP digest, and the grade source identifies the graded run IDs. Attempts and retries within one session never count as separate runs.

Repeat-run cells report the median and population standard deviation for GAP, ORIENT, and POS. Attempted and graded counts remain separate so failed exports do not disappear. Task, scoring version, kit, prompt, model/tool, and harness cohort may not be mixed inside one cell.

8. Planned work

  1. Populate new frontier cells under the v0.10 repeat-run rule. Retrospective reruns of legacy cells are a separate, budgeted study.
  2. A random-floor baseline: grade shuffled positions to establish a true floor beneath the current relative median of about 47 mm.
  3. Principal-axes orientation refinement (OBB or PCA) to catch off-axis rotations the current axis-aligned metric misses.
  4. A second task on a different machine type, to support a claim of generality.
  5. The manufacturing-tolerance axis, to grade the real-world plus-or-minus of made parts (a separate axis the AI is not tested on yet).
